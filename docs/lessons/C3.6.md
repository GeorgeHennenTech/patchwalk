## You will be able to
Predict what a cutoff envelope does on a high-pass or band-pass filter.

## Hear it first
The same envelope on a low-pass, a high-pass and a band-pass filter.

## The idea
The envelope always does the same thing: it pushes the cutoff up and lets it fall. What you hear depends on which side of the cutoff the filter keeps. On a low-pass, a falling cutoff takes brightness away. On a high-pass, a falling cutoff lets the low end back in, so the body of the note arrives late. On a band-pass, the slice that gets through slides downward, which sounds like a vowel changing.

## Steps
| Do | On screen | Check | You should hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strike a few keys on the low-pass filter. Envelope 2 is already sent to the cutoff. | Filter type, Filter curve, Envelope 2 drawing | Low-pass, two notes played | Bright start, closing down. |
| Switch to High-pass and strike again. | Filter type, Filter curve, Envelope 2 drawing | High-pass, two notes played | A thin start that fills in: the weight of the note arrives a moment late. |
| Switch to Band-pass and raise Resonance to about 60%. | Filter type, Resonance, Filter curve, Envelope 2 drawing | Band-pass, resonance 50% or more, a note played | A “yow”: one narrow slice sliding down through the harmonics. |
| Raise the Cutoff to about 1 kHz and turn Envelope 2’s Amount below zero. | Cutoff, Env 2 amount, Filter curve, Envelope 2 drawing | Band-pass, cutoff 700 Hz or above, amount below zero, a note played | The slice sliding upward: “woy”. |

## Recap
- The envelope moves the cutoff the same way on every filter shape.
- On a high-pass, a falling cutoff brings the low end in late.
- On a band-pass, it slides a slice through the sound, like a vowel.
