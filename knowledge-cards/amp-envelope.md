## What it is
The amp envelope sets how loud a note is over its lifetime: how quickly it arrives, where it settles while the key is held, and how it fades after the key is let go.

## Why it works
Attack is the time taken to reach full level, decay is the fall from that peak to the sustain level, sustain is the level held for as long as the key is down, and release is the fade after the key is released. The ear identifies an instrument largely from the first instant of a note, so the attack does more to define character than any other stage. The curve of each stage changes how the level moves through that time: a curve that drops fast and then lingers feels percussive, an even one feels mechanical.

## Listen for
A very short attack gives a click or a hard front edge; lengthening it softens the note into a swell. A low sustain with a short decay turns a held key into a pluck. A short release cuts notes off cleanly; a long one lets them overlap and smear together.

## Goes with
In a mono patch with legato, the envelope does not restart between overlapping notes, so the attack is only heard on the first note of a phrase. Long releases build up under reverb and delay.
