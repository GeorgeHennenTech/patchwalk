## What it is
An LFO is routed to an oscillator's level, so the loudness rises and falls in a repeating pattern. That effect is called tremolo.

## Why it works
Loudness is the most direct thing the ear tracks, so even a small regular change is easy to hear as a pulse. A smooth LFO shape gives a gentle throb, like the motor effect on an electric piano; a sharper shape chops the sound into a rhythm. When the route is bipolar the level moves both above and below its resting point, so the average loudness stays about the same.

## Listen for
A steady pulsing on held notes, with the pitch and tone staying put. Raising the amount deepens the dips; raising the rate turns a throb into a flutter.

## Goes with
Smoothing the LFO softens the edges of each pulse. Tremolo is often put under a macro so its depth or speed can be changed while playing. It is different from vibrato, which moves pitch, and from a filter wobble, which moves brightness.
