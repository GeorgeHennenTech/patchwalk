## You will be able to
Thicken a single oscillator with unison and judge when it turns from wide to blurry.

## Hear it first
One voice, then seven voices spread apart.

## The idea
Unison is the detuning trick done inside one oscillator: several copies, spread slightly in pitch. With many copies the individual beats average out into a smooth thickness. Spreading the copies across left and right adds width.

## Steps
| Do | On screen | Check | You should hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hold a chord with one voice. | Voices, detune, spread | Voices is 1 | A plain chord |
| Raise Voices to 3 with detune at zero. | Same | 3 voices, detune 0 | Only louder |
| Raise Detune slowly. | Same | Detune above zero | A swirl appearing |
| Raise Voices to 7. | Same | 7 voices | Smoother and thicker |
| Push Detune until the chord sounds sour, then back off. | Same | Detune reduced from its peak | The edge of "too much" |
| Raise Spread. | Same | Spread above zero | The sound opening to the sides |

## Ear check
One voice or several? Then: which example has more detune?

## In Serum
From Init, give Osc A three or more unison voices. Save and drop it: the app checks the stored voice count.

## Recap
- Unison stacks detuned copies.
- More voices sound smoother.
- Too much detune blurs the pitch.

## Guide notes
Comment on where the learner stopped. Mention that thick unison on bass notes muddies the low end, pointing ahead to C1.7.
