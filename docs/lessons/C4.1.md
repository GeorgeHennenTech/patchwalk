## You will be able to
Explain what an LFO is, and set its rate and shape while it moves a control.

## Hear it first
A steady note, then the same note with an LFO moving its level slowly, then quickly.

## The idea
An LFO is a low-frequency oscillator: the same repeating shapes you met in the oscillator chapter, but far too slow to hear as a pitch. You do not listen to it. You send it to a control, and it turns that control up and down for you, over and over. Rate is how many times a second it repeats. Shape is the path it takes: a sine glides, a square jumps between two positions, a saw ramps and snaps back. An envelope moves a control once per note; an LFO keeps going.

## Steps
| Do | On screen | Check | You should hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Switch on Hold and play a note. LFO 1 is not connected to anything yet. | LFO 1 sends to | LFO 1 sent to nothing, note held | A steady note. |
| Set LFO 1’s “Sends to” to Osc A level. | LFO 1 sends to, LFO 1 rate, A level | LFO 1 sent to Osc A level | The note swelling and fading twice a second. Osc A’s Level knob now wears a cyan ring. |
| Slow the Rate down to about half a cycle a second. | LFO 1 rate, A level, Level meter | Rate between 0.3 and 0.7 Hz | A slow swell you can count: up for a second, down for a second. |
| Speed it up to about 8 cycles a second. | LFO 1 rate, A level, Level meter | Rate between 6 and 10 Hz | A flutter. |
| Bring the Rate back to about 2, then try each Shape. Finish on Saw. | LFO 1 shape, LFO 1 rate | Rate between 1 and 4 Hz, shape changed at least twice, now on Saw | Sine glides, triangle is similar, square chops the note on and off, saw ramps up and drops. |
| Now turn the Rate all the way up. | LFO 1 rate, Spectrum | Rate at 25 Hz or more | The movement blurs into a buzz that is part of the tone. |

## In Serum
Serum’s LFOs are in the row under the oscillators, as here. Drag LFO 1 onto a LEVEL knob and change RATE. Serum’s LFO shapes can also be drawn, which this synth cannot do yet.

In the guide: LFOs, from p. 187; modes FREE, RETRIG and ENVELOPE on p. 191.

## Recap
- An LFO is an oscillator too slow to hear, used to move a control.
- Rate is how often it repeats; shape is the path it takes.
- Very fast movement stops sounding like movement and becomes part of the tone.

## Guide notes
Uses Hold: an LFO needs a sustained note. The practice synth's LFOs restart with every note and run in Hz only. Say when fast movement stops being heard as movement.
