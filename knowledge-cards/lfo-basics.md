## What it is
An LFO is a low-frequency oscillator: a repeating shape that is too slow to hear as a tone. It makes no sound itself. It is used to move another setting up and down over and over.

## Why it works
A static sound is quickly ignored by the ear; a setting that keeps moving holds attention. The LFO's shape sets how the setting moves, smoothly, in a ramp, or in steps. Its rate sets how fast the pattern repeats, either in time with the song's tempo or free-running. Its trigger mode sets whether the pattern restarts with each note or carries on regardless, which decides whether every note sounds the same.

## Listen for
An LFO is only audible through what it is routed to. Once routed, listen for a pattern that repeats at a steady rate.

## Goes with
A rise or delay setting fades the movement in after a note starts. Smoothing rounds off sharp corners in the shape. A drawn shape can act like a small repeating envelope.
