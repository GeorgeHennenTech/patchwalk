## You will be able to
Recognise sine, triangle, saw and square by ear and by their spectra, and say roughly what each contains.

## Hear it first
One note played through all four shapes in turn.

## The idea
The oscillator repeats a shape, and the shape decides the harmonics. A sine is a single harmonic. A triangle adds a few quiet odd harmonics. A square has strong odd harmonics, which sounds hollow. A saw has every harmonic, which sounds bright and full. Most classic synth sounds start from a saw or a square because there is plenty to carve away later.

## Steps
| Do | On screen | Check | You should hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hold a note on Sine. | Wave selector, scope, spectrum | Sine selected, note held | Pure and soft; one peak |
| Switch to Triangle. | Same | Triangle selected | Slightly brighter, still soft |
| Switch to Square. | Same | Square selected | Hollow, like a clarinet; every other peak missing |
| Switch to Saw. | Same | Saw selected | Bright and buzzy; every peak present |
| Play a low note and a high note on Saw, then on Sine. | Same | Two notes on each | Saw stays bright; a low sine is hard to hear on small speakers |

## Ear check
One shape is played: which of the four is it? (Eight rounds, different pitches.)

## In Serum
Load Init and step Osc A's wavetable position through the default table's shapes while holding a note. No check: this is listening only.

In the guide: Using Wavetable Oscillators, pp. 37-45. The position control is labelled WT POS.

## Recap
- Sine: one harmonic. Triangle: a few quiet odd ones.
- Square: strong odd harmonics, hollow.
- Saw: all harmonics, bright.

## Guide notes
Square and triangle are the usual confusion; direct attention to brightness. If the learner is on laptop speakers, warn that low sines may seem to disappear and why.
