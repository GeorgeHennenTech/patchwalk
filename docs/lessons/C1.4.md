## You will be able to
Layer a second oscillator, balance it against the first, and make one key play an interval.

## Hear it first
Oscillator A alone, then B added an octave above, then B a fifth above.

## The idea
A second oscillator is a second source in the same patch. At the same pitch it thickens the tone. An octave apart it adds weight or sparkle. At other intervals one key plays two notes. Their levels are a small mix: the louder one sets the character.

## Steps
| Do | On screen | Check | You should hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hold a note with only Osc A. | Osc A, Osc B (off) | B is off | The single tone |
| Switch on Osc B at the same pitch and shape. | Osc B power, level | B on, same octave and semitone | Louder, slightly fuller |
| Set Osc B one octave up. | Osc B octave | B octave is +1 | Added brightness on top |
| Lower Osc B's level until it supports and does not dominate. | Osc B level | B at least 6 dB below A | A still the main tone |
| Set Osc B's octave back to 0 and its semitone to +7. | Osc B semitone | B octave 0, semitone +7 | One key playing a fifth |

## Ear check
Is this one oscillator or two? Then: is the second an octave above, a fifth above, or at the same pitch?

## In Serum
From Init, switch on Osc B and set it an octave above Osc A. Save and drop it: the app checks that B is on and its stored octave.

In the guide: Using Wavetable Oscillators, pp. 37-57; LEVEL is on p. 57.

## Recap
- A second oscillator thickens, adds register, or adds an interval.
- Their levels are a mix.
- The louder oscillator defines the sound.

## Guide notes
Two identical oscillators with fixed phase can sound thinner or louder depending on phase; if the learner notices, that is lesson C1.8. Comment on the balance they chose.
