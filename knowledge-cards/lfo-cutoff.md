## What it is
A low-frequency oscillator, a slow repeating shape, is routed to the filter cutoff. The filter opens and closes over and over in the pattern of that shape.

## Why it works
The cutoff decides how many upper harmonics get through, so moving it rhythmically makes the tone alternate between dark and bright. The LFO's shape sets the feel: a smooth shape gives a rounded wah, a ramp gives a repeated pluck, a stepped or random shape gives jumps. The rate sets how fast the cycle repeats and the route amount sets how wide the sweep is.

## Listen for
A regular "wow" or wobble in brightness on a held note. Synced to tempo it locks to the beat; free-running it drifts against it. Too much amount with a low resting cutoff can make the sound vanish at the bottom of each cycle.

## Goes with
Resonance sharpens the sweep into a vocal quality. Changing the LFO rate with a macro or the mod wheel is a common way to perform this kind of movement.
