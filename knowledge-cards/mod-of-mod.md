## What it is
The destination of a route is not a sound setting but a setting of another modulator: an LFO's rate or smoothing, or an envelope's time or curve.

## Why it works
An LFO or envelope on its own repeats exactly, and the ear soon stops noticing an exact repeat. Changing the modulator's own settings, either by hand through a macro or automatically with a second modulator, makes the movement itself evolve: it can speed up, soften, or change shape over time. It also turns a fixed setting into a performance control, so a player can push a wobble faster or make a pluck longer in the moment.

## Listen for
The movement, not just the tone, should change as the controlling macro or modulator moves: a pulse that accelerates, a sweep whose edges soften, a decay that lengthens.

## Goes with
This is one of the main ways a patch is made to feel hand-played. If a movement seems inconsistent when you copy a patch, look for a route that targets the modulator producing it.
