## What it is
Ring modulation multiplies two signals together. In place of the two notes you put in, you get their sum and their difference.

## Why it works
Multiplying two waves is the same as making one control the other's level, very fast and in both directions. For two pure tones the arithmetic is exact: the output contains one tone at the two frequencies added together and one at the two frequencies subtracted, and neither of the originals. With an octave or another simple ratio between the inputs, the sum and difference land on notes that fit and the sound stays musical. With any other ratio they land off the scale, which is why ring modulation is known for metallic, bell-like and robotic tones.

## Listen for
The original note disappearing as the amount reaches full, replaced by two others. Moving one oscillator's pitch should send the two new tones in opposite directions: one rises as the other falls.

## Goes with
A mix control that fades between the plain oscillator and the multiplied one, so a little of the effect adds an inharmonic edge without losing the note. Sine waves keep it clean; every extra harmonic in either input is added to and subtracted from every harmonic in the other.
