# Learning research behind the lesson and guide design

Owner's request, 14 Nov 2026: back the design of lessons and guide answers with published research, and
tailor the courses to a learner with ADHD (the owner has ADHD and called this "super super important").

**What this is.** Design rules drawn from articles retrieved from PubMed, each with its DOI. It is guidance
for building the app, not medical advice and not a treatment for ADHD.

**How it was read.** About 150 records were retrieved across some 30 searches; titles and the closing part
of each abstract were read, not full papers. Roughly 45 bore on lesson design; the rest were off-topic
(prevalence, drug trials, animal work). PubMed's tool returned at most 20 records per request, so about 25
search hits were never retrieved. PubMed mainly covers medicine and neuroscience, so education research is
thin there. **Most ADHD studies are on children and adolescents; evidence in adults is sparse**, so the
ADHD rules below are reasonable inferences to be tested on the owner, not proven facts about adults.

## A. Rules for every learner

| Rule | Evidence |
|---|---|

| Always give the right answer after a quiz item: a wrong choice left uncorrected can be learned | Marsh 2012 https://doi.org/10.1080/09658211.2012.708757 |
| Being tested helps learning of what comes next | Pastötter 2014 https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00286 |
| Bring material back after a gap; longer gaps for longer memory | Cepeda 2006 https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.132.3.354 |
| Mix kinds when learning to tell things apart by ear or eye; do not mix when learning from text | Brunmair 2019 https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000209 ; across senses: Abel 2023 https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.13270 |
| Practise one idea on varied material so it carries over | Raviv 2022 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2022.03.007 |

## B. Rules for a learner with ADHD

| Rule | Evidence | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Pay off at once: every step gives a sound or a visible result within seconds; never a long read before doing | Waiting is felt as costly: Sonuga-Barke 2003 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2003.08.005 , 2005 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biopsych.2004.09.008 ; delayed rewards discounted steeply: Levitt 2020 https://doi.org/10.1007/7854_2020_128 , Bickel 2019 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.copsyc.2019.01.005 | Theory and reviews; how rewards change behaviour in ADHD is less clear than the theories say: Luman 2004 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2004.11.001 |
| No punishment: no lost streaks, no red failure screens | van der Oord 2020 https://doi.org/10.1007/s10567-020-00327-z | Review, children |
| Lead with interest, not prizes: say why the lesson matters for the music the learner wants | Morsink 2021 https://doi.org/10.1177/10870547211050948 | Review |
| Make time visible: minutes left, steps left, clear stopping points, resume where you were | Time is misjudged: Zheng 2020 https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054720978557 ; adults name it too: Chua 2026 https://doi.org/10.1017/ipm.2026.10175 | Meta-analysis in children; interviews in adults |
| Take load off memory instead of training it: one instruction at a time, goal always in view, earlier settings shown, never "remember this for later" | Support beats strain: Gathercole 2006 https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7610.2005.01446.x ; memory training does not carry over: Melby-Lervåg 2012 https://doi.org/10.1037/a0028228 | Review; meta-analysis |
| Give the structure explicitly: a map of the lesson, numbered recap, "you are here" | Recall in ADHD students was less well ordered: Stern 2023 https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1301726 | One study |
| Recall the whole lesson at the end in one go; avoid many small quizzes mid-lesson | Whole-text recall beat section recall; note neither beat re-studying in this study, so keep it short and low-stakes: Stern 2023 (above) | One study |
| Reading comfort setting: wider spacing, shorter lines | Spacing and attention interact in reading comprehension: Stern 2012 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ridd.2012.08.021 | One study, adolescents |
| Optional pink or white noise while reading, off by default, set per person, never during listening tasks | Small benefit in ADHD, harm without ADHD: Nigg 2024 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2023.12.014 ; Söderlund 2007 https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1469-7610.2007.01749.x | Meta-analysis, youth; safe loudness not established |
| Easy re-entry after drifting: on return show "you were here" and the last thing done, without comment | Noticing one's own drifting reduces its cost: Franklin 2016 https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054714543494 | One study |
| Do not design for marathon sessions; offer breaks and stopping points | Hyperfocus is common but tied to distractibility and less likely in study settings: Hupfeld 2018 https://doi.org/10.1007/s12402-018-0272-y , Zhang 2023 https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0292215 , Groen 2020 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ridd.2020.103789 | Surveys |
| Suggest a short movement break between lessons | Exercise had large effects on self-control and flexible thinking: Qiu 2023 https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajp.2023.103692 | Meta-analysis, children |
| Show strengths: what the learner is good at, not only what is left | Using one's strengths went with better well-being: Hargitai 2025 https://doi.org/10.1017/S0033291725101232 | One study, adults |
| Game elements stay light | Games for ADHD improved test scores more than everyday behaviour: Lee 2025 https://doi.org/10.1007/s00787-025-02788-5 ; Lin 2025 https://doi.org/10.2196/60937 | Reviews, children |

## C. What already fits, and what to build

Already in the app: one instruction per step with only the needed controls on screen; an instant tick when a
step's target is met; sound within seconds of every action; short lessons with a time estimate; "hear it
first"; by-ear checkpoints; lessons in any order.

To build, in suggested order:
1. **Focus mode** (a switch, per person): time and steps left always visible, a "good place to stop" marker
   after each lesson, "you were here" on return, reading comfort setting, movement-break suggestion.
2. **End-of-lesson recall** in place of the recap being read: two or three questions answered from memory,
   then the recap shown as feedback. One go, at the end.
3. **Coming back**: recall questions resurface after days, then weeks.
4. **Mixed by-ear checkpoints**: a checkpoint that mixes kinds from earlier chapters (filter, envelope, LFO)
   instead of one kind at a time.
5. **"Why this matters"**: one line per lesson tying it to music the learner wants to make.
6. **Fading guidance**: later lessons in a chapter give the goal and let the learner find the steps.
7. **Optional noise** while reading (pink noise from the synth's own generator), off by default.
8. Guide answers: the layout in section A (one-line answer first, one highlight system, pictures by their
   words, go-deeper questions, one check question).

Open question for the owner: which of the ADHD rules match their own experience. The research is mostly on
children, so their report outranks it.
