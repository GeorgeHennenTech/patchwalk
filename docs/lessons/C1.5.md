## You will be able to
Make two oscillators beat against each other and control how fast the movement is.

## Hear it first
Two oscillators exactly in tune, then nudged apart: a still tone turning into a slow roll.

## The idea
Two tones at almost the same frequency drift in and out of step. In step they add; out of step they cancel. You hear that as a swell that repeats at a rate equal to the difference between the two frequencies. A wider gap beats faster, and the same gap in cents beats faster on higher notes.

## Steps
| Do | On screen | Check | You should hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hold a low note with both oscillators at the same pitch. | Both fine controls, beat-rate readout | Both fine at 0 | A steady tone |
| Move Osc A fine to -5 cents and Osc B to +5. | Same | Gap of 8 to 12 cents | A slow swell |
| Widen the gap until the readout shows about 4 beats per second. | Same | 3.5 to 4.5 beats per second | A faster roll |
| Keep the gap and play an octave higher. | Same | A higher note played | The roll speeding up |
| Find a gap you like for a bass sound. | Same | Any gap over 4 cents | A restless, moving low tone |

## Ear check
Is this tone still or beating? Then: which of two examples beats faster?

## In Serum
From Init, switch on Osc B and fine-tune A and B in opposite directions. Save and drop it: the app checks that B is on and the two fine values differ.

## Recap
- Two near-equal pitches beat.
- Beat speed is the frequency difference.
- Wider gaps and higher notes beat faster.

## Guide notes
Tell the learner the beat rate they landed on. If the gap passes roughly a quarter-tone it stops sounding like one note; name that as "now it sounds out of tune".
