## What it is
Three controls set where an oscillator sits relative to the key you play. Octave moves it in whole octaves, semitones in keyboard steps, and fine tune in hundredths of a step, called cents.

## Why it works
An octave doubles or halves the frequency, so the note keeps its name but changes register; this is how the same patch becomes a bass or a lead. Semitone offsets turn one key into an interval when another oscillator stays put, which is how a single key can play a fifth or a chord. Fine tune is too small to hear as a different note; its use is to put two sources slightly out of step with each other.

## Listen for
Octave changes sound like the same instrument played higher or lower. A semitone offset against a second oscillator sounds like two notes. Fine tune alone is nearly inaudible until another oscillator is there to compare with.

## Goes with
A global transpose shifts everything together and is often used to place a patch in a useful keyboard range.
