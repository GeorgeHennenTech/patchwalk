## What it is
A filter's shape says which part of the sound it keeps. Low-pass keeps what is below the cutoff, high-pass keeps what is above it, band-pass keeps a slice around it, and notch removes a slice around it. Slope says how quickly the filter turns things down once they are past the cutoff.

## Why it works
No filter is a wall. Past the cutoff, each harmonic is turned down a little more than the one before it, and slope is the rate of that fall, counted in decibels per octave. A gentle slope leaves a trail of quiet upper harmonics, so the sound darkens softly. A steep slope removes them almost completely, so the edge of the sound is more definite. Band-pass and notch are the same idea applied on both sides of one frequency, and resonance sets how narrow that slice is.

## Listen for
High-pass thins a sound from the bottom: the weight goes first and fizz is left. Band-pass sounds small and boxy, like a voice on a telephone. A notch is hard to hear when it stands still and obvious when it moves. Switching from a gentle to a steep low-pass at the same cutoff sounds like the cutoff moved down.

## Goes with
High-pass is the usual way to clear low rumble out of pads, leads and noise so they leave room for a bass. Band-pass with a moving cutoff gives talking, vowel-like sweeps. A steep low-pass with resonance is the classic choice for basses and plucks.
