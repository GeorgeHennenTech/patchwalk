## What it is
Frequency modulation uses one oscillator to push another's pitch up and down very quickly. The oscillator doing the pushing does not have to be heard at all; what you hear is the oscillator being pushed.

## Why it works
A slow wobble in pitch is heard as vibrato. Speed the wobble up until it is as fast as a note and the ear stops hearing movement and hears a new tone instead: extra harmonics appear on both sides of the played pitch, spaced by the speed of the pushing oscillator. The amount sets how many of them there are and how strong, so more amount means brighter. When the two oscillators are in a simple ratio, such as the same note or an octave apart, the new harmonics line up with the note and the result sounds tuned. When they are not, the harmonics fall between and the tone turns clangorous, like a bell or struck metal.

## Listen for
At a low amount, a plain tone gaining an edge. As the amount rises, a brighter and more complex tone whose harmonics rise and fall unevenly, where a filter opening would brighten smoothly. Changing the pushing oscillator's pitch should change the character completely, from smooth to bell-like.

## Goes with
An envelope on the amount, so the tone is bright at the start of a note and settles: the classic electric piano and plucked bass. Sine waves on both oscillators give the clearest result; rich waves get noisy quickly.
