## What it is
Most synthesizers build a sound in the same order. Oscillators make a raw, bright tone. A filter removes part of it. An amplifier, shaped by an envelope, decides how loud it is from moment to moment. Effects then process the result.

## Why it works
This is called subtractive synthesis: start with more harmonics than you want and carve away. It mirrors how many real instruments behave, where a rich vibration is shaped by the body of the instrument. Keeping the stages in a fixed order makes a patch readable, because each stage has one job and you can ask of any sound which stage is responsible for what you hear.

## Listen for
On an untouched starting patch you hear only the first stage: a plain, steady, bright tone that starts and stops abruptly. Every later stage takes that tone further from where it began.

## Goes with
Modulation sits beside the path, not in it: envelopes, LFOs and macros do not make sound, they move the settings of the stages that do.
