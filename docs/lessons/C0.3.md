## You will be able to
Read a level meter, say what a decibel change means in practice, and set a safe listening level.

## Hear it first
The same note at three levels, each step the same number of decibels apart.

## The idea
Level is how big the wave is. It is measured in decibels, a scale built on ratios, so equal decibel steps sound like equal steps in loudness. The meter shows level; your ears judge loudness, and the two do not always agree, because brighter and denser sounds seem louder at the same level.

## Steps
| Do | On screen | Check | You should hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hold a note and watch the meter. | Level meter in dB | Note held | A steady reading |
| Lower the level by 6 dB. | Level control | 5 to 7 dB below the start | Clearly quieter, not half as loud |
| Lower it another 6 dB. | Same | 11 to 13 dB below the start | About half as loud as the start |
| Bring it back to the start. | Same | Within 1 dB of the start | The original loudness |
| Set your device volume so this level is comfortable to hear for minutes. | Reminder card | Acknowledged | A level you could talk over |

## Ear check
Two versions of a note: which is louder? Then: is the difference small (about 3 dB) or large (about 10 dB)?

## In Serum
None for this lesson.

## Recap
- Decibels describe ratios; equal steps sound equal.
- Roughly 10 dB sounds like twice or half as loud.
- Loud listening tires your ears and hides detail.

## Guide notes
The "half as loud" figure is a rule of thumb, not a law; say so if asked. If the learner keeps the level high, remind them once about listening level and then leave it.
