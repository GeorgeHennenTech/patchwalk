## You will be able to
Push a sound into the filter hard enough to distort it, and know what that adds.

## Hear it first
A filtered bass note clean, then driven hard.

## The idea
Drive turns the sound up on its way into the filter until the wave no longer fits and its peaks are squashed flat. Changing a wave’s shape adds harmonics, so the sound gets thicker and grittier. Because the squashing happens before the filter, the filter then smooths the new harmonics. Dark filter, heavy drive: warm and thick. Open filter, heavy drive: harsh.

## Steps
| Do | On screen | Check | You should hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Switch on Hold and play a low note with Drive at zero. | Drive, Scope | Drive at 0, note held | A clean, rounded bass tone. |
| Raise Drive to about 50%. | Drive, Scope, Spectrum | Drive between 40% and 60% | Thicker and a little louder, with some grit. |
| Push Drive all the way up. | Drive, Scope, Spectrum | Drive at 90% or more | Squashed and aggressive. |
| Leave Drive high and sweep the cutoff. | Drive, Cutoff, Filter curve | Drive at 50% or more, cutoff swept across two octaves | The filter taming the grit when it closes and letting it out when it opens. |
| Switch on Osc B and set its semitone to +7, so one key plays two notes into the drive. | Drive, Osc B, B semitone | Osc B on at +7 semitones, drive at 50% or more | A growl: rougher than either note alone. |
| Pull Drive back to an amount you would actually use. | Drive, Cutoff, Filter curve | Drive between 10% and 70%, moved in this step | Weight and a little grit, without the mush. |

## Recap
- Drive squashes the wave before the filter, which adds harmonics.
- The filter then shapes those new harmonics.
- Single notes stay clear under drive; intervals and chords turn rough.

## Guide notes
Explain why two notes into the drive sound rough. The practice synth's drive is a simple soft clip; do not claim it models any Serum filter.
