## What it is
Effects process the sound one after another, top to bottom in the rack. Each effect receives whatever the one before it produced.

## Why it works
Most effects do not commute: swapping two of them gives a different result. Distortion after reverb distorts the whole wash; reverb after distortion puts a clean space around a distorted sound. A common order is tone and dynamics first, such as distortion, EQ and compression, then modulation such as chorus, then time-based effects such as delay and reverb, and level or width last. Departing from that order is a deliberate choice with an audible result.

## Listen for
When copying a patch, an effect in the wrong place can make the whole thing sound unlike the original even with every knob matched.

## Goes with
A splitter lets different frequency ranges go through different effects, which is how a bass can be distorted on top while its low end stays clean.
