## You will be able to
Lock an LFO to a tempo, so its movement falls on the beat and follows the tempo when it changes.

## Hear it first
A wobble once a beat, then twice a beat, then three times a beat, all at the same tempo.

## The idea
So far an LFO’s speed has been a number of cycles per second. Music is counted in beats, and a wobble at an arbitrary speed drifts against the beat. Setting the speed in beats ties it to the tempo: once a beat, twice a beat, once a bar. Change the tempo and a synced LFO changes with it, staying in the same place in the bar. A third of a beat gives the rolling, three-against-the-beat feel called a triplet. This LFO is set to run free, which here means it keeps time with the click however late or early you press a key.

## Steps
| Do | On screen | Check | You should hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Switch on the Click, then switch on Hold and play a low note. | Click, Tempo, LFO 1 speed in | Click on, speed in Hz, note held | A wobble at its own speed, drifting against the click. |
| Change LFO 1’s “Speed in” to Beats. | Click, LFO 1 speed in, LFO 1 repeats every, Filter curve | Speed in beats, repeating every 1 beat | One wobble for every click, landing on it. |
| Set “Repeats every” to half a beat. | Click, LFO 1 repeats every, Filter curve | Repeating every half a beat | Two wobbles per click. |
| Now a third of a beat. | Click, LFO 1 repeats every, Filter curve | Repeating every third of a beat | Three wobbles per click: a rolling triplet feel. |
| Go back to 1 beat, then turn the Tempo down below 100 and up above 140. | Click, Tempo, LFO 1 repeats every, Filter curve | Repeating every 1 beat; tempo taken below 100 and above 140 | The click and the wobble slowing down and speeding up together. |
| Set “Repeats every” to 4 beats. | Click, LFO 1 repeats every, Filter curve | Repeating every 4 beats | One slow sweep per bar, turning over on the higher click. |

## In Serum
Each Serum LFO has a BPM switch beside RATE; with it on, RATE reads in beat divisions, with TRIP and DOT buttons for triplet and dotted values (guide, LFO section from p. 187). Serum follows your DAW’s tempo.

In the guide: LFOs, from p. 187 (BPM, RATE, TRIP, DOT).

## Recap
- An LFO can be set in beats instead of cycles per second.
- A synced LFO follows the tempo and stays in place in the bar.
- A third of a beat is a triplet.

## Guide notes
Uses the click and the Tempo knob. The LFO is set to Free so it stays on the click whenever a key is pressed. The practice synth has its own tempo; Serum follows the DAW's.
