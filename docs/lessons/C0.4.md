## You will be able to
Explain why two sounds at the same pitch and loudness can sound different, and read that difference on a spectrum.

## Hear it first
The same note played as a pure tone, then as a bright buzzy tone.

## The idea
Almost every pitched sound is a stack of simpler tones called harmonics: one at the pitch you hear, and others at whole multiples of it. Which harmonics are present, and how strong each is, gives a sound its colour. The spectrum view shows them as peaks from left (low) to right (high).

## Steps
| Do | On screen | Check | You should hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hold a note on the pure tone. | Spectrum | Note held | A soft, hollow tone; one peak on the spectrum |
| Switch to the bright tone. | Two-way tone switch | Bright tone selected | A buzzy tone; a row of peaks |
| Count the first five peaks and hover each. | Harmonic readout on hover | Five peaks inspected | Nothing new |
| Change the note. | Keyboard | A different note played | The whole row of peaks moving together |
| Switch back and forth while holding. | Tone switch | Switched at least four times | Same pitch, different colour |

## Ear check
Two notes at the same pitch: are they the same colour or different? Then: which one is brighter?

## In Serum
None for this lesson.

## Recap
- A pitched sound is a stack of harmonics.
- The mix of harmonics is the tone colour.
- More and stronger high harmonics sound brighter.

## Guide notes
Do not name wave shapes yet; that is the next chapter. If asked what the peaks are, they are sine tones at multiples of the note's frequency.
