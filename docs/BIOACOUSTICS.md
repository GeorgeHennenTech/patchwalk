# Track R: Bioacoustics and the psychedelic ear

Requested by the owner on 13 Nov 2026: "whatever bioacoustics you think may help improve the psychedelic
aspect of my music that I'm going for as a Tipper fan". Status: **planned, nothing built.**

## What this track is, and what it is not

Bioacoustics is the study of how living things make and hear sound. It earns a place here for a practical
reason: the sounds people describe as organic, alien, liquid or "alive" are mostly sounds that follow rules
animals' voices and ears follow. Learn the rules and those qualities can be designed on purpose.

Three limits, to keep the track honest:

- It teaches mechanisms and how to imitate them. It does not claim to know how any artist made a particular
  sound, Tipper included.
- "Psychedelic" here means perceptual effects that can be demonstrated and heard: ambiguity, illusion,
  sounds that seem to change while staying the same. The track makes no claim that any sound alters the
  brain or consciousness. Where a popular claim is weak (binaural beats and "brainwave entrainment" is the
  usual one), the lesson says so.
- Each lesson's science must come from a text in the library. **None is in the library yet**, so these
  lessons can be planned and their sound-making parts built, but the explanations wait for sources. Suggested
  to the owner: Fletcher, *Acoustic Systems in Biology*; Bradbury and Vehrencamp, *Principles of Animal
  Communication*; Bregman, *Auditory Scene Analysis*; Moore, *An Introduction to the Psychology of Hearing*;
  Deutsch (ed.), *The Psychology of Music*; Krause, *The Great Animal Orchestra* (for the soundscape idea).

It leans on other tracks: B (hearing and psychoacoustics), Q (physical modelling, for tubes and membranes),
D (formants, granular, FM) and the practice synth's modulation chapters.

## Lessons (about 45)

### R1. How animals make sound (10)
| # | Lesson | You learn | Imitated with |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1.1 | Source and filter | A buzzing source and a tube that shapes it: the plan behind most voices | Oscillator into band-pass filters |

| R1.3 | Size from sound | Longer throats put the peaks lower: why big things sound big even at the same pitch | Shifting formants without shifting pitch |
| R1.4 | Two voices at once | A songbird's two-sided organ can sing two unrelated notes together | Two oscillators with separate pitch movement |
| R1.5 | Scraping: crickets and cicadas | A row of teeth dragged across an edge: a train of clicks through a resonator | Impulse trains into a resonant filter |
| R1.6 | Drumming and clicks | Fish swim bladders, woodpeckers, dolphin clicks: struck bodies | Short bursts into modelled resonators |
| R1.7 | Roughness and non-linear voices | Screams, growls and roars: when a voice breaks into noise and sub-tones on purpose | Drive, FM, ring modulation, sub-octaves |
| R1.8 | Purring and pulsing | Slow on-off pulsing of a tone, and why it reads as an animal and not a machine | Amplitude modulation at pulse rates |
| R1.9 | Water and bubbles | The pitch of a bubble; why liquid sounds chirp upward | Rising pitch envelopes on short sines |
| R1.C | Checkpoint | Build a creature voice with a throat, a size and a mood | |

## What the app needs for this track
- **Formant filters** (two or three band-passes in parallel) on the practice synth. Small job; unlocks R1.1 to R1.3.
- **Audio-rate modulation and FM** (already on the build list as chapter 6). Unlocks R1.7, R2.2.
- **Drawn pitch curves** (drawable LFOs and envelopes, on the build list). Unlocks R2.3.
- **A stereo output per ear** and headphone prompts, for R4.8 and R5.3 to R5.5.
- **Several voices at once with their own clocks**, for the chorus chapter. This is the mini DAW's job.
- **Physical modelling** (track Q) for tubes, membranes and struck bodies.
- A "Listening lab" page type for R4 and R5: fixed demonstrations with a question, not synth lessons.
