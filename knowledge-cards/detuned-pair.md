## What it is
Two oscillators play the same note, but one is nudged a little flat and the other a little sharp. Neither sounds out of tune alone; together they make a slow, rolling movement. On low notes this is the classic "reese" bass.

## Why it works
Two waves at almost the same frequency drift in and out of step. When their peaks line up they add and the sound swells; when they oppose they cancel and it thins. That swell-and-thin cycle is called beating, and its speed is the difference between the two frequencies, so a wider gap beats faster. Because every harmonic has its own gap, the upper harmonics beat faster than the fundamental and the tone seems to churn.

## Listen for
A slow phasing or growling motion on a single held note, with no LFO involved. Small gaps give a gentle shimmer, larger gaps a restless, seasick wobble. The beat speed changes with the note you play.

## Goes with
A clean sub oscillator underneath keeps the low end steady while the pair moves above it. Distortion after the pair exaggerates the movement, and a filter or EQ sweep across it brings out different parts of the churn.
