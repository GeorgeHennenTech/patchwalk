## You will be able to
Add a resonant peak at the cutoff, hear what it does to a sweep, and judge when it is too much.

## Hear it first
A filtered note, then the same note with a strong resonant peak.

## The idea
Resonance boosts a narrow band right at the cutoff. On the curve it is a bump that grows into a spike. Whatever harmonic sits under the bump is pushed forward, so the filter gains a pitch of its own, and a sweep turns from a plain fade into a whistle or a squelch. On this synth, as on many, the rest of the sound is turned down as the peak grows.

## Steps
| Do | On screen | Check | You should hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Switch on Hold and play a note. The filter is already on, with no resonance. | Resonance, Filter curve | Resonance at 0, note held | A rounded tone. |
| Raise Resonance to about 50%. | Resonance, Filter curve | Resonance between 40% and 60% | A nasal edge; a bump appears on the curve at the cutoff. |
| Sweep the cutoff up and down with the resonance left where it is. | Cutoff, Resonance, Filter curve | Cutoff swept across two octaves, resonance still at 40% or more | A “wow” with an edge that follows the cutoff, picking out harmonics one at a time. |
| Push Resonance past 85%. | Cutoff, Resonance, Filter curve, Level meter | Resonance at 85% or more | A sharp whistle, and less body underneath it. |
| Bring it back to a level that adds character without taking over. | Cutoff, Resonance, Filter curve | Resonance brought back to between 15% and 50% | Edge, but the note itself is still in charge. |

## Recap
- Resonance is a boost right at the cutoff.
- It makes a sweep whistle or squelch, because it picks out harmonics one by one.
- A lot of resonance thins the body of the sound.

## Guide notes
Report the real height of the peak in dB. If the learner leaves resonance very high, point out that the body of the sound has been turned down.
