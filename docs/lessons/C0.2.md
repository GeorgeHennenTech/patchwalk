## You will be able to
Explain pitch as how fast a wave repeats, and predict what doubling or halving that speed does.

## Hear it first
A tone sweeping slowly from low to high while the scope shows the wave squeezing together.

## The idea
A pitched sound is a shape that repeats. The number of repeats per second is its frequency, measured in hertz. More repeats per second sounds higher. Double the frequency and you hear the same note one octave up; halve it and you hear it one octave down.

## Steps
| Do | On screen | Check | You should hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hold a note and look at the scope. | Scope | Note held for two seconds | A steady tone; a wave standing still on the scope |
| Drag the frequency slider up slowly. | Frequency slider with a Hz readout | Frequency raised by at least an octave | Pitch rising; waves on the scope getting narrower |
| Set the frequency to 220 Hz. | Same | 215 to 225 Hz | A low A |
| Now set it to 440 Hz. | Same | 435 to 445 Hz | The same note, one octave higher |
| Predict the next octave up, then set it. | Same | 870 to 890 Hz | Another octave up |

## Ear check
Two tones are played. Which is higher? Then: are these two tones an octave apart, or not? (Five rounds each.)

## In Serum
None for this lesson.

## Recap
- Frequency is repeats per second, in hertz.
- Higher frequency sounds higher in pitch.
- Each octave is a doubling.

## Guide notes
Learners often expect equal steps in hertz to sound like equal steps in pitch; point out that 220 to 440 and 440 to 880 sound like the same jump. If they overshoot a target, tell them by how much in hertz.
