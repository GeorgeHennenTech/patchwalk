## What it is
Drive turns the sound up on its way into the filter until it distorts. Key tracking makes the cutoff follow the key that is played.

## Why it works
When a wave is pushed past what the circuit can carry, its peaks are flattened. A flattened wave is a different shape, and a different shape means new harmonics, so the sound gains thickness and grit. Because this happens before the filtering, the filter then shapes the new harmonics along with the old ones. Key tracking exists because a cutoff is a fixed frequency while notes are not: a cutoff that leaves a low note bright can sit below most of a high note's harmonics. Moving the cutoff by the same interval as the note keeps the same number of harmonics on every key.

## Listen for
A driven single note sounds fuller and slightly louder, then squashed and aggressive as the amount rises. Two notes driven together sound rough, because distortion also creates new tones between and below them. Without key tracking, a melody gets duller as it climbs; with full tracking it stays evenly bright.

## Goes with
Drive suits basses and leads that play one note at a time; with chords it turns to mush quickly. Key tracking is usually on for anything melodic, and off or partial for sounds where high notes should soften. Full tracking with high resonance lets the filter's own ringing be played in tune.
