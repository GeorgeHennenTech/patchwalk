## You will be able to
Add a sub for weight and noise for air, and keep both in proportion.

## Hear it first
A bright tone alone, then with a sub underneath, then with a layer of noise on top.

## The idea
A sub oscillator is a simple low tone under the main pitch. It anchors the low end and stays steady even when the main oscillators are detuned. Noise has no pitch: its energy is spread across the spectrum, so it reads as breath, air or grit.

## Steps
| Do | On screen | Check | You should hear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hold a note in the middle of the keyboard. | Sub, noise (both off), spectrum | Both off | The main tone |
| Switch on the sub, one octave down. | Sub power, octave, level | Sub on at -1 octave | Weight underneath; a new peak on the left |
| Lower the main oscillator briefly to hear the sub alone, then restore it. | Main level | Main lowered then restored | A soft low tone on its own |
| Switch on white noise at a low level. | Noise power, type, level | Noise on, well below the main level | A hiss; the spectrum filling in everywhere |
| Switch the noise to pink. | Noise type | Pink selected | A darker, softer hiss |

## Ear check
Is there a sub under this sound? Then: is the noise white or pink?

## Recap
- The sub adds steady weight.
- Noise adds unpitched texture.
- Both work best quiet.

## Guide notes
On small speakers the sub may be inaudible; point to the spectrum peak as proof it is there. Comment if the noise is louder than the tone.
